# PFIZER INC (PFE) — 2026-W40

Pfizer's revenue is flat and reported earnings swing between profit and loss, but trailing free cash flow was large and the chief executive and two directors each bought about $1 million of shares in August 2026. Valuation and the one-year return sit mid-range, so the expectations gap is small: the case is cash flow, not growth.

## Why it may be mispriced
- Three insiders bought about $1 million each within a week while net income was swinging between profit and loss [m:insider:1e0c6f7c-0d5c-4ca3-8fbe-fc0e0af40af7] [m:insider:7b5cf7cf-bd18-40d3-9b24-2b63111122ad] [m:insider:0ff1c3db-fe65-4f42-80a8-d02b5a1f7f0b].
- C1 puts EV/sales of 3.46 at the 45.9th percentile of the company's own 522-week history [c:C1].
- The trailing one-year return was 1.6%, at the 45.8th percentile of the 409-company comparison set [c:C3].

## Inferences
- Inference: S4 fired because reported net income fell much faster than operating cash flow, which also fell; that is not an improvement in cash generation.
- Inference: three senior insiders buying similar amounts within a week suggests a shared view that the shares are cheap against the cash the business produces.
- Inference: with revenue flat, the case rests on cash generation holding up; the pack has no product-level data to test that.

## Assumptions
- Assumption: operating cash flow stays near its recent level through 2028.
- Assumption: the charges that pushed net income into losses are not a sign of lasting damage to the business.
- Assumption: net debt falls from free cash flow rather than rising with new acquisitions.
