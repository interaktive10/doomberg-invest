# Matador Resources Co (MTDR) — 2026-W40

Matador's chief executive bought repeatedly in August 2026, and the reverse DCF reads the price as implying a steady decline in revenue. Against that, receivables rose much faster than revenue (red flag R2), the stock is not cheap against its own history and it rose over the past year. Commodity prices decide most of this.

## Facts
- Trailing-twelve-month revenue was $3.645 billion, 3.6% below a year earlier [m:revenue_ttm] [m:revenue_ttm_yoy].
- Revenue rose to $1.186 billion in the quarter ended 2026-06-30 from $671.6 million in the quarter ended 2026-03-31, and was up 32.5% year over year [m:revenue:2026-06-30] [m:revenue:2026-03-31] [m:revenue_q_yoy].
- The trailing operating margin was 32.2% and ROIC was 10.9% [m:op_margin_ttm] [m:roic].
- Total debt rose to $4.216 billion at 2026-06-30 from $3.469 billion at 2026-03-31, and net debt was $4.19 billion [m:total_debt:2026-06-30] [m:total_debt:2026-03-31] [m:net_debt].
- Cash conversion was 3.60, up from 2.83 a year earlier, and S4 fired at strength 1.0 [s:S4].
- Chairman and chief executive Joseph Wm Foran bought on several days between 2026-08-10 and 2026-08-26, including 10,000 shares for $521,600 on 2026-08-12 [m:insider:db4d7891-6536-42cf-9ab3-e64690f83d6f].
- Chief financial officer Christopher P. Calvert bought 2,500 shares for $141,600 on 2026-08-27, and S7 fired at strength 0.83 with 3 insiders [m:insider:1eb596e7-22b4-40c7-ac06-0b3155a63ae4] [s:S7].
- Red flag R2 fired: receivables grew 21.5% while revenue fell 3.6% [s:R2].

## Why it may be mispriced
- C2 scores 100: the enterprise value implies revenue shrinking 11.7% a year, against 21.2% growth from the evidence formula [c:C2].

## Inferences
- Inference: the chief executive's repeated purchases through August 2026 are the clearest signal in the pack.
- Inference: for an oil and gas producer, operating cash flow normally runs well above net income because of depletion charges, so S4 at full strength says less here than it would for most companies.
- Inference: the reverse DCF reads the price as pricing in a steady decline; whether that is too pessimistic depends on commodity prices and reinvestment, which the pack does not cover.

## Assumptions
- Assumption: oil and gas prices stay near the levels of the past year.
- Assumption: the jump in revenue in the June 2026 quarter is not a one-off.
- Assumption: the rise in receivables reflects timing, not collection problems.

## Risks
- Red flag R2: receivables grew much faster than revenue, which cut the Evidence score [s:R2] [c:evidence].
- C1 puts EV/EBIT at the 74th percentile of the company's own history, so it is not cheap against itself [c:C1].
- The stock already rose 18.8% over the trailing year [c:C3].
- Debt rose sharply in the June 2026 quarter [m:total_debt:2026-06-30] [m:total_debt:2026-03-31].
